# Black-Ink Bestiary — Style Bible

## Brand Promise

Every page should feel like it belongs in the same book:

> **A recognizable fantasy monster, in a believable habitat, drawn as clean black-ink line art with generous space to color.**

## Visual Target

- Portrait page.
- Pure black ink on white.
- Strong, clean silhouette.
- Bold outer contours.
- Lighter interior lines.
- Large uninterrupted white areas.
- Medium-low detail density.
- Environment supports the monster without competing with it.
- Fantasy tone aimed above preschool level.
- Clear enough for casual colorers; interesting enough for adults.

## Target Ranges

These are guidance targets, not rigid scoring rules.

- Open white area: **high**.
- Background complexity: **low to medium**.
- Crosshatching: **none or nearly none**.
- Solid-black shadow masses: **minimal**.
- Outer contour strength: **high**.
- Interior line strength: **medium-low**.
- Tiny texture density: **low**.
- Number of focal subjects: usually **1 monster**.

## Composition Rules

1. Monster is the dominant subject.
2. Habitat is immediately readable.
3. One clear action/moment.
4. Avoid crowd scenes unless the creature is specifically a swarm.
5. Leave breathing room around the silhouette.
6. Do not fill every blank region with stones, grass, cracks, debris, scales, fur strokes, or decorative marks.
7. Important scene objects must be readable at thumbnail size.

## Monster Identity

A page fails if the creature could easily be mistaken for a different monster.

Each page spec must define:
- silhouette cues
- anatomy cues
- signature feature(s)
- prohibited look-alikes

## Reference Image Rule

Reference images exist to answer:

> What anatomical and silhouette traits make this creature recognizable?

Use them for:
- head shape
- body proportions
- tail, horns, wings, limbs, claws, or other signature anatomy
- silhouette
- species-defining equipment when appropriate

Do **not** copy:
- pose
- composition
- lighting
- rendering
- colors
- background
- decorative details unique to the source artwork

Black-Ink house style always overrides the rendering style of the reference.

## Habitat Rule

The monster should appear where it plausibly lives, hunts, lairs, feeds, guards, or naturally operates.

The habitat should help tell the story:
- kobold → trapped tunnel / lair
- roper → stalagmite cavern
- rust monster → abandoned armory / metal-rich ruin
- ankheg → earth tunnel / burrow
- mimic → treasure room / dungeon furnishing

The background is context, not the star.

## Coloring Rule

Ask:

> Would a person enjoy coloring this page, or would the line density feel like homework?

If the answer is “homework,” simplify it.

Prefer:
- broad armor panels
- clean body regions
- simple stone blocks
- large foliage shapes
- open wings
- readable scales rather than hundreds of scales

Avoid:
- dense hair/fur strokes across the entire body
- tiny brick cracks everywhere
- full-surface stippling
- heavy crosshatching
- painterly black shadow fills
- micro-texture repeated across all surfaces

## Not Allowed

- grayscale wash
- color
- text inside the art
- labels
- logos
- decorative page frames
- fake parchment background
- photorealism
- stick figures
- childish “cute” reinterpretations unless a page spec explicitly asks for cute
- unrelated props added just to fill space

## Modification Rule

When a page is close, MODIFY should preserve good work.

Every modify instruction should separate:

### Preserve
What must not move/change.

### Change
What specifically needs improvement.

### Avoid
What the next pass must not introduce.

A MODIFY pass should be surgical. It should not restart the whole composition unless the reviewer requests it.

## Regenerate Rule

Use REGENERATE when:
- monster identity is wrong
- anatomy is fundamentally broken
- composition fails
- habitat is wrong
- page is stylistically unusable
- targeted editing would cost more effort than a fresh attempt

## Golden Five

Before scaling Tome I, approve five reference pages that demonstrate the style across different creature types.

Suggested categories:
1. Humanoid — Kobold Warrior
2. Ooze/object — Gelatinous Cube or Mimic
3. Arthropod — Ankheg
4. Large creature — Troll or Bulette
5. Unusual anatomy — Roper

These become style references, not composition templates.

## Reviewer Test

Before APPROVE & LOCK:

- Is the monster unmistakable?
- Is the habitat appropriate?
- Is there one clear moment?
- Is the silhouette strong?
- Is there enough open space to color?
- Are interior details lighter than the outside contour?
- Is the background simple enough?
- Is there any gray wash or heavy black fill?
- Would this page look consistent beside the approved reference pages?
- Would I personally want to color it?

If any major answer is “no,” MODIFY or REGENERATE.
